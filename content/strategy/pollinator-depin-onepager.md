Small houses for wild bees with sensors inside. Each one measures local
biodiversity where it stands. Together they make a map. Built on the
HiveBits network, next to our hives.

## Where this comes from

Many people asked us for indoor hives, so they could have bees at home and
be part of HiveBits. We do not want to do that. Bees belong outside. But the
question was right: people want a way to join that is not only buying honey
or a token.

This is that way. A house for wild bees you put in your garden, on your
balcony, or at your company. It does not need a beekeeper. It needs sun, a
wall, and WiFi.

## Why wild bees

Honey bees are one species. There are hundreds of wild bee species around
every apiary and they do a big part of the pollination. Almost nobody
measures them. Bee hotels are sold everywhere, but a study of 600 of them
found more wasps than bees inside, and no data that they help at all.

Research also says honey bees and wild bees share the same flowers, and
that too many hives in one place can push wild bees out. A beekeeper who
wants to do this right needs to know what is around the apiary. We have 300
hives on our farm. We want that number to be in balance with everything
that flies around it, and we want to show it, not just say it.

## How it fits HiveBits

HiveBits already tracks our hives: humidity, temperature, weight, with our
own sensors. The pollinator house is the same system in a smaller box,
pointed at the bees we do not keep. First houses go next to our hives on
the farm, so every HiveBits farm gets a biodiversity reading around it.
Then houses go to people and companies, and the map grows beyond our farms.

For HiveBits this means: more devices in the network, a farm that can show
it is pollinator positive with data, and a product for people who cannot
keep bees.

## What it measures

Inside the house: temperature, humidity, a counter at the entrance, a daily
photo of the nesting tubes. Outside: temperature, humidity, pressure,
light, rainfall.

## What a host gets

- The house with sensors and the first bees (cocoons), so year one is not empty.
- The house in the app: live data, daily photo, "first bee moved in" messages, and your spot on the biodiversity map.
- Network rewards for keeping the house online and sending data.
- A yearly service: send the nesting block back, get it cleaned and refilled.
- Companies also get a biodiversity report for ESG and a live page for employees.

## Who pays

- Companies that adopt houses for ESG. This works today: 3Bee in Italy sells it to 500+ brands.
- Food brands and growers that need proof their supply chain is pollinator safe. M&S and Importaco already pay for pollinator sensors on farms.
- Later: certification and EU nature credits, which will need proof that is not collected by hand.

Raw weather data alone has no buyer, WeatherXM showed that. Pollinator
activity with weather next to it, for a specific place, does. And the
bigger the map, the more it is worth.
